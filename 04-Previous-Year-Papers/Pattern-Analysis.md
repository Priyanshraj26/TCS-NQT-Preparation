- Hard questions are rare (2-3 per paper) and typically appear in:

## 6. Time Management Strategy Per Section

### Recommended Time Allocation (Total: 120 minutes)

```
Section                    Questions   Time    Per Question   Strategy
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Quantitative Aptitude      20          25 min  ~75 sec        Do easy ones first (1 min each),
                                                              skip hard DI/geometry if stuck

Logical Reasoning          15          20 min  ~80 sec        Series & coding-decoding first,
                                                              arrangements last (time-heavy)

Verbal Ability             15          12 min  ~48 sec        Fastest section - RC first,
                                                              grammar is quick

Programming Logic          10          13 min  ~78 sec        Output prediction = quick wins,
                                                              DSA theory needs thought

Coding                     2           45 min  ~22 min each   Easy one first (15 min),
                                                              Medium one next (25-30 min)

Buffer / Review            -           5 min   -              Review flagged questions
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TOTAL                      62          120 min
```

### Section-wise Strategy

#### Quantitative Aptitude (25 min)
- **First pass (15 min):** Solve all Easy questions (percentages, averages, simple interest, basic geometry)
- **Second pass (10 min):** Attempt Medium questions (mixtures, pipes, DI)
- **Skip:** Hard questions that take more than 2 minutes -- come back if time permits

#### Logical Reasoning (20 min)
- **First pass (10 min):** Series, coding-decoding, odd-one-out, analogies
- **Second pass (7 min):** Blood relations, direction sense, syllogisms
- **Last (3 min):** Complex seating arrangements (only if time allows)

#### Verbal Ability (12 min)
- **Speed section:** Most questions are direct and require minimal calculation
- **Do RC passages first** (read quickly, answer based on direct references)
- **Grammar and vocabulary** are quick -- trust your instinct, do not overthink

#### Programming Logic (13 min)
- **Output prediction (7 min):** Trace carefully, watch for post-increment, pointer arithmetic, mutable defaults
- **DSA theory (6 min):** These are either known or not -- do not spend too long

#### Coding (45 min)
- **Easy question first (15 min):** Aim for 100% test cases. Clean, simple code.
- **Medium question (25 min):** Focus on correctness first, then edge cases. Even partial marks (some test cases) count.
- **Buffer (5 min):** Debug and handle edge cases

### Critical Tips
1. **No negative marking** -- attempt EVERY question, even if you guess
2. **Do not get stuck** on any single question for more than 2 minutes in MCQ sections
3. **Coding section** carries disproportionate weight -- allocate sufficient time
4. **Start with your strongest section** to build confidence and save time
5. **Flag and return** strategy works well since there are no sectional time limits

## 7. Year-over-Year Insights

### 2022 Observations
- Most straightforward paper of the three years
- Heavy emphasis on basic formulas and direct application
- Coding questions were classic standard problems (FizzBuzz, Spiral Matrix)

### 2023 Observations
- Slight increase in medium-difficulty questions
- More emphasis on application-based reasoning
- Coding questions tested fundamental algorithms (Kadane's, string processing)

### 2024 Observations
- Duration increased to 120 minutes (from 110)
- More questions requiring multi-step reasoning
- Introduction of hash map-based coding problems
- Greater variety in logical reasoning question types

### What to Expect Going Forward
- Expect **62 questions** in **120 minutes** format to continue
- Difficulty will likely stay at current level or increase marginally
- Coding questions will increasingly test hash maps, sliding window, and two-pointer techniques
- Programming logic may include more Python-specific questions
- Data Interpretation sets may become more complex
